> **In short.** Choose WordPress for a content site (pages, blog, forms) that you want to edit yourself at a low starting cost. Choose custom development when the site has its own logic: stock, accounts, calculations, integrations, approval flows. If unsure, start on WordPress and move only the logic into a separate app.

## The difference in one sentence

WordPress is a ready-made system you fill with content and plugins. A custom site is code written for your case, where every feature exists because you need it.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Criterion | WordPress | Custom website |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Time to launch | Days to 3 weeks | 3–12 weeks |
| Content editing | Familiar panel, many people know it | Panel built around your work |
| Speed | Good with caching and a light theme | Very good, no extra code |
| Security | Depends on updates and plugins | Small, controlled surface |
| Special features | Through plugins, with limits | Anything |
| Long-term cost | Updates, plugin licences | Maintenance on request |

## When WordPress is the right call

- A presentation or services site with a blog
- Your team publishes new articles or pages often
- You need standard plugins: forms, SEO, galleries, simple bookings
- The starting budget is tight and you want to validate the offer first

## When a custom website pays off

- Data changes often and has rules: stock, prices, availability
- Users have accounts and different roles
- The site calculates something: leasing, quotes, rates
- It must connect to other systems: accounting, CRM, external APIs

For [SwissCars](/en/work/swisscars), stock changes weekly, each car has 30–60 photos and leasing options, and marking a car "Sold" must remove it from the list within seconds. On WordPress that would take 5–6 plugins wired together. The custom app does all of it from one panel.

## The middle ground: both

Many businesses don't have to choose. The presentation site and blog stay on WordPress, and the logic (calculator, customer account, configurator) becomes a small app linked to the site. You pay for custom only where custom is needed.

## Common mistakes

### A plugin for everything
Every plugin is someone else's code, updated on someone else's schedule. Past 20–25 plugins, sites get slow and hard to update.

### Custom for a content site
If the site only has pages and articles, custom development adds cost without benefit.

### No maintenance plan
Both options need updates. The difference is who does them and how often.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WordPress secure?
Yes, when it's kept updated, uses few plugins, has daily backups and good hosting. Most problems come from old or abandoned plugins.

### Can I move from WordPress to custom later?
Yes. Content can be exported and URLs kept through redirects, so you keep your Google rankings.

### Which is better for SEO?
Both can rank very well. Speed, page structure and content matter, not the platform.
