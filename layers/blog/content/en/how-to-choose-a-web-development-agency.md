> **In short.** Before signing, ask who owns the code and accounts, how payments map to deliveries, what happens if the project slips, and who maintains it after launch. A good agency answers specifically and in writing. Vague answers here mean a vague project.

## Ownership and access

### 1. Whose name are the domain, hosting and code under?
The right answer: yours. If the agency keeps them in its own accounts, leaving becomes a negotiation.

### 2. Do I get the full source code?
Ask for repository access from day one, not at the end. You'll also see the pace of work.

### 3. Which paid licences does the project use?
Themes, plugins, external services. Each has a yearly cost that should be in the proposal.

## Timelines and money

### 4. How are payments tied to deliveries?
Deposit, test version, launch. Each payment matches something you can see and test.

### 5. What happens if something new turns up mid-project?
Ask for a real example. At [Bloom](/en/projects/bloom), an issue found in load tests before 8 March was fixed without moving the launch. You want an agency with stories like that, not one that says it never happens.

### 6. How are new requests handled along the way?
Right: estimated separately, in writing, before they're built. Wrong: "we'll do it and see".

## Way of working

### 7. How often will I see progress?
A test version every one to two weeks is a healthy pace.

### 8. Who is my contact and how fast do they reply?
A name, not a generic inbox. A reply time, for example 24 hours.

### 9. Who writes the copy and takes the photos?
If it isn't clear from the start, the project ends up waiting on content.

## After launch

### 10. What warranty do I get for bugs?
A clear period, for example 30 days, when bugs are fixed for free.

### 11. What does maintenance cost and what does it include?
Updates, backups, monitoring. Ask for the list, not just the price.

### 12. Can I move to another agency without losing anything?
If questions 1 and 2 got a "yes", so does this one.

## Red flags

- A price given before asking what the site needs to do
- The full amount paid upfront
- A portfolio with no live links or client names
- Refusing to put timelines in the contract

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a freelancer or an agency safer?
It depends on the project. For a small site, a good freelancer is enough. For an app with several modules, it matters that someone can pick up the work if one person is away.

### How detailed should the proposal be?
Detailed enough to check at the end that you got everything: pages or modules, integrations, timelines, what's not included.
